# Blend three slow drifts into one book

**Verdict.** Pre-register one long-short US equity book whose single score per stock is an equal-weight average of three standardized, residualized components: a link-propagation score (the partner-return signal on the point-in-time link graph), a section-level Filing Change score, and an opportunistic insider-buy score. A continuous volatility and trend-state scaler from the ETF Trend signal sets gross exposure, and a small set of vetoes carries the useful parts of the other strategies. Event Ripple's language-model brain, standalone forced-seller trading and binary contracts stay out of the first test. The reason is structural: the three kept components all exploit slow information diffusion, they trade at monthly tranches that a small account can reach, and they are likely only weakly correlated (nobody has measured that, so the first run must). Each also covers the others' known holes. Link momentum has decayed since publication, so Filing Change and insider events add independent breadth. Filing Change's evidence is concentrated on the short leg and in mid caps, and the link score helps pick which shorts are borrowable and not crowded. Insider buys are event-sparse and long-only, which pairs with the short-heavy Filing Change. The published numbers are all in-sample and decayed: plain customer momentum fell from 1.52%/month to 0.62%/month after 2005 and is insignificant value-weighted ([Pinchuk](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.11394)); the S&P 100 Filing Change replication found nothing ([iqueipopg](https://github.com/iqueipopg/lazy-prices)); and the average anomaly nets about zero after borrow fees ([Muravyev et al., JF 2025, via search summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13501)). The blend is a bet on breadth and netted turnover (the evidence for combining is real, [AQR, via search summary](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Long-Only-Style-Investing)), not on any one signal being strong. It can still fail on costs alone, and that is an acceptable outcome of a pre-registered test. Everything below is a draft for the operator; nothing is registered or backtested.

## What the evidence says about each of the eight strategies

Only link momentum, Filing Change and insider buys have enough evidence to be components, and every one of them has decayed or is contested. Two readings of this table drive the design. First, the effects share a mechanism: investors process slow, indirect or unglamorous information late (a partner's return, a changed risk-factor paragraph, an insider's purchase). Link momentum and Filing Change are explicitly attention stories, and the spillover shrinks with analyst coverage and institutional ownership ([Menzly-Ozbas, S](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i4p1555-1580.html)). That is the structural edge to build on. Second, the effects fail the same way: the strongest versions sit in small, under-covered names where borrow and spreads bite, and published versions lose roughly half or more of their return (McLean-Pontiff: 26% out of sample, 58% post-publication, [S](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)). A design that ignores the cost problem repeats the average anomaly's fate.

## Why a composite score, not eight sleeves

The research on combining says integrate, regularize and net. AQR found that picking stocks with simultaneously strong multi-style exposure returned 11.0% and Sharpe 0.68 against 9.3% and 0.49 for a mix of stand-alone factor portfolios, because integration avoids names with offsetting exposures ([AQR, S](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Long-Only-Style-Investing)). Gu-Kelly-Xiu show the other half: with 900+ predictors unconstrained OLS has deeply negative out-of-sample R2 while shrinkage and dimension reduction work ([Gu-Kelly-Xiu, NBER w25398, P](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25398/w25398.pdf)). Three signals with event-sparse histories cannot support fitted regression weights, so the first test uses fixed equal weights. Netting matters too: WorldQuant's published alphas show a mean pairwise correlation near 16% and gain from crossing opposing trades inside one combined portfolio ([Kakushadze, P](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00991)). The cost of this approach is that effective breadth is below the signal count, and with three components the Grinold gain is at most about the square root of three if score correlations are near zero (inference from IR = IC x sqrt(breadth); collapses toward one signal if they are correlated).

The design is also what the plan already asks for. `strategies.md` says the fund's durable edge is the number of independent validated signals, measured through the effective-signal count, and that a strategy adding no spanning alpha against the reference book is not admitted. The blend is registered as one new pre-registration, so it answers the question "does the composite span the reference book" directly, and its drop-one ablations are the attribution.

## The proposed strategy: Connected-Drift Book

**Stage 1, per-component scores.** Each component is winsorized, cross-sectionally ranked to a z-score, and residualized against market beta, size, industry and the firm's own trailing one-month return, so none carries a known factor twice.

The link component is the plan's edge-weighted mean of linked firms' past-month returns, built from edges (a) customer and supplier disclosures, (b) 10 nearest 10-K text peers and (d) 13F common ownership, equal-weighted across sources as in `strategies.md`. Three fixes come from the evidence: partner returns are measured as residual returns net of market and industry (the DeepSeek day moved whole themes together, so raw partner moves are mostly a common factor, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/27/nvidia-sheds-almost-600-billion-in-market-cap-biggest-drop-ever.html)); both the own-firm and partner momentum are neutralized (Burt-Hrdlicka); and customer and supplier edges carry a relation type so complementary and competitive links are not given the same sign (the Apple case above).

The Filing Change component is the deterministic section-level similarity of each 10-K and 10-Q to its prior-year counterpart on Item 1A and MD&A, with litigation and CEO/CFO language as the highest-prior sub-scores. A low-similarity name gets a negative z, a high-similarity name a small positive one, since the paper finds the long leg's alpha reverts to zero and the short leg's persists.

The insider component fires only where a Form 4 open-market purchase by an opportunistic insider (no calendar-month habit over three or more years) exists, in the trailing month, and is zero elsewhere. Sales and routine trades never enter.

**Stage 2, composite and gating.** The score is `(z_link + z_filing + z_insider) / 3`, with a missing component equal to 0 so event signals move only the names where they fire. Gating then does four jobs:
- **Agreement gate.** A candidate is dropped when any single component contradicts the composite's sign beyond a threshold fixed at registration. This is the integration benefit AQR describes; the threshold is a design parameter, proposed at one standard deviation, and is the operator's to set.
- **Short veto, distress.** Filing Change shorts overlap with distress and delisting: changers predict bankruptcy ([NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)). The Shumway delisting correction applies to prices, and shorts need the borrow flag and the plan's crowding filter and a market cap of at least $1B.
- **Short veto, forced selling.** A short candidate whose 13F ownership shows concentrated distressed-seller exposure is vetoed. This is the one place the forced-seller strategy earns its keep: it is the rebound risk on the short side, and the 13F ownership edges already exist. It is a veto, not a signal, so it adds no variant.
- **Reversal control.** Extreme own one-month moves are neutralized in Stage 1; Savor and Chan find that moves with news drift and moves without news reverse ([Chan, S](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v70y2003i2p223-260.html)), so a no-news extreme is the case where a momentum-style link score is most likely to be wrong. A reliable no-news flag on the free feed is an open question (Gaps).

**Stage 3, execution and exposure.** Names are traded through the existing tranche portfolio: monthly signal from the close, next-session execution, one third of the book re-ranked each month with a three-month hold, a no-trade band and a position cap. Slow tranching is also the cost lever: Novy-Marx and Velikov find buy/hold bands are the cheapest mitigation ([S](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20721/w20721.pdf)), and Hoberg-Phillips find less-visible peer shocks transmit for up to 12 months. Gross exposure is scaled by the ETF Trend and volatility state, continuously and with a capped rate of change, to target the plan's 10% annual volatility. The purpose is the Daniel-Moskowitz panic state (down market, high volatility, rebound), where momentum-style long-short books crash ([Columbia, S](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/momentum-crashes)). The notes found no source that quantifies this overlay on a market-neutral stock book, so it is a design inference with a cost: it will sit out part of any V-rebound.

**Weights.** One third each at the composite; edge sources equal-weighted inside the link component. Equal weights are chosen because fitting weights on event-sparse components is the failure mode in the combination literature; a ridge-toward-equal variant is the only registered alternative.

## Build order against what exists

Existing: backtest runner, tranche portfolio, shorts and margin ledger, ETF Trend signal, text peers, 13F ownership edges, link-graph store, GDELT GKG reader, Form 4 events. Missing: customer and supplier extractor, text-change signal.

1. **Text-change signal.** Section-level similarity on Item 1A and MD&A with litigation and CEO/CFO sub-scores. It is the first missing module and a blocker for the filing component. Extraction rates matter: the one replication extracted Item 1A for 92% and Item 7 for 77% of filings.
2. **Customer and supplier extractor.** A deterministic parser with an alias table and a fail-closed match. Before committing, measure coverage by year on EDGAR; the notes could not resolve whether names or only the existence of 10% customers are disclosed after the 2020 amendments. If coverage collapses, register without edge (a) and say so, rather than adding it later.
3. **Opportunistic classifier on the Form 4 events.** Flag purchases by insiders without a three-year calendar-month habit; compute entry timestamps.
4. **Composite builder.** Residualization, equal-weight composite, agreement gate and vetoes, using the link-graph store, text peers and 13F edges.
5. **ETF Trend scaler.** Reuse the signal as a continuous, rate-capped exposure input to the harness; confirm it runs against the US-set margin ledger.
6. **Pre-registration, then one run through the harness and ledger,** then the diagnostic runs, then the spanning test and the shadow gate. The first backtest can happen only after the operator approves the registration.

Data needs per part: link edges need point-in-time 10-K text and 13F (free); the news edge needs the GDELT GKG reader (its coverage is poor for company-level events, [GDELT overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDELT_Project)); the shared-analyst-coverage edge that dominates the literature needs paid I/B/E/S and is replaced by free proxies per `strategies.md`; Form 4 needs filing timestamps; delisting returns and borrow data need a vendor, and missing delisted prices trigger the plan's 5% void rule and paid-data trigger. These are stage settings, not design anchors: the design does not change if the paid sources arrive.

## Honest uncertainty

Three things limit confidence. First, provenance: only a few figures come from full papers (Cohen-Malloy-Nguyen, Gu-Kelly-Xiu, Bailey-Lopez de Prado, Harvey-Liu-Zhu, Pinchuk's replication, Lakonishok-Lee). The link-momentum notes could not parse the Hoberg-Phillips, Ali-Hirshleifer and Bloomberg PDFs. The forced-seller figures (-10.1%, 14.57%), the Muravyev borrow-fee result, McLean-Pontiff magnitudes and the AQR integration comparison come from search summaries, and the notes say all liquidity figures should be re-verified before they enter a registration. The news and event notes are abstract-level for FinRipple and Glasserman-Lin.

Second, the trend and risk notes are thin. They are all search summaries; they retrieved no fund letters and no verified CTA return series; the post-2010 weakness comes partly from an unreviewed 2026 preprint; and they found no source quantifying a trend overlay on a market-neutral stock book. The overlay's net benefit is therefore a hypothesis the diagnostic run must test; the drawdown reduction for momentum books is supported (Barroso-Santa-Clara cut maximum drawdown from -96.69% to -45.20% in the original sample, [S](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256017573_Momentum_Has_Its_Moments)), but Cederburg and others find vol-managed gains largely vanish out of sample and after costs ([S](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342497053_Understanding_Volatility-Managed_Portfolios)).

Third, the blend itself has no peer-reviewed validation. No source measures the correlation among link, filing and insider scores; no source gives net-of-cost, post-2015, tradable-universe evidence for link propagation or filing change; the day-2-onward insider drift in the book's cap range is unknown; and no source tested decay for these three specifically. The 50% decay budget is the McLean-Pontiff prior, applied by plan rule, not measured here. Steelman against the blend: the average anomaly nets zero after short costs, so a book built from three decayed anomalies could simply net zero; and an equal-weight composite of weak signals has no proven advantage over the single best one. The pre-registered spanning test and drop-one ablations exist to answer exactly that.

## Conclusion

The research does not support picking a winner among the eight. It supports retiring the framing: two of the eight (forced-seller trading, binary contracts) contribute at most a veto or nothing, one (Event Ripple's brain) cannot be evaluated on history by the plan's own contamination rules, and the remaining signals are weak enough that only their combination, traded as one netted book, has a plausible path to net alpha. The most useful finding for the fund is structural: the slow-diffusion signals fail in the same places (small names, shorts, post-publication) and win from the same mechanism, which argues for a single universe, a single cost model and a single set of vetoes rather than independent sleeves.

The first thing the harness should report is not Sharpe but the correlation matrix and the effective signal count `(Σλ)² / Σλ²` of the three components. If the components turn out highly correlated, the blend collapses to one signal and the right action is to keep only the stronger one; if they are near-orthogonal, the case for the composite is made before a return is computed.
